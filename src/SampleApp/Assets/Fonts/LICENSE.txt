The fonts in this directory are carried by the demonstration app so that the PDFs
it produces are laid out the same way on every machine, rather than with whatever
the machine happens to have installed. All four families are licensed under the
SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1, whose text is reproduced once below and
covers all of them.

LiberationSans-Regular.ttf, LiberationSans-Bold.ttf,
LiberationSans-Italic.ttf, LiberationSans-BoldItalic.ttf
LiberationSerif-Regular.ttf, LiberationSerif-Bold.ttf,
LiberationSerif-Italic.ttf, LiberationSerif-BoldItalic.ttf
	Digitized data copyright (c) 2010 Google Corporation
		with Reserved Font Arimo, Tinos and Cousine.
	Copyright (c) 2012 Red Hat, Inc.
		with Reserved Font Name Liberation.
	The Serif faces are from liberation-fonts-ttf-2.1.5. Sans carries the
	metrics of Arial and Serif those of Times New Roman, so a demo laid out
	here has the proportions a reader expects of those two faces.

SourceCodePro-Regular.otf
	(c) 2023 Adobe (http://www.adobe.com/), with Reserved Font Name 'Source'.
	All Rights Reserved. Source is a trademark of Adobe in the United States
	and/or other countries.
	Carried for two reasons: it is the monospaced face the newspaper and
	magazine demos set code and captions in, and its outlines are PostScript
	(CFF) rather than TrueType, which is a different embedding path in the
	library and one that cannot be subsetted. Only a regular face ships, so a
	request for bold or italic comes back simulated - which the Fonts demo
	shows deliberately.

NotoSansArabic-Regular.ttf
	Copyright 2022 The Noto Project Authors
	(https://github.com/notofonts/arabic), with Reserved Font Name 'Noto'.
	Carried because none of the three families above has a single Arabic
	glyph, and the International demo is about text the Latin faces cannot
	draw: letters that change shape according to what they sit between, a
	line that reads right to left, and a character falling back to the face
	that has it. Shaped through PdfPinata.HarfBuzz - without a shaper the
	letters appear in their isolated forms, which the demo also shows,
	because that contrast is the point.
